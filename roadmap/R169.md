# R169 — Two subscriptions as one pool

Colony picks models by their cost and intelligence return within one program. This item extends that to both,
Claude and Codex, so work goes where it is best done for what it costs. Work is handed from one program to the
other where that pays, and stays put where the handoff's own cost would eat the gain.

## Where it came from

The person's direction (rule ua1aecd29, their words in R169.words.md). On 2026-11-16 the person's view and colony's
had drifted apart:

- Colony's own rule held Codex in reserve, for second opinions only.
- By the person's direction, two projects were building with Codex to use the less expensive subscription. That
  was about 95% of Codex's tokens that week, in about 130 build runs. Second opinions were a small part: 65 in
  seven weeks, tapering to 1 that week.
- Both weeks ran out: Codex's whole week, and Fable's own week inside Claude's.
- Each project chose its program for itself, so colony could neither see the split nor pace it.

The person's reading is that the question isn't "reserve Codex or not". There is one pool across two providers,
to be scaled by return as Auto already scales models, with work crossing where it pays.

## What it measured before design (2026-11-17)

- **Getting a helper up to speed costs about 40% of its run** on either program, measured as the share spent
  before its first edit. Medians: Codex builds 43% (25 runs, quartiles 16–76%); Claude build helpers 37%
  (67 runs, quartiles 22–67%). Part of that reading would happen anyway, but the agent that already holds the
  context pays none of it.
- **What Fable's own week went on**, from its reset (Fri Nov 13, 3 PM) to its limit (Mon Nov 16, 11:47 PM), about
  $1,070 at API prices in under 3½ days: main agents 60%, the step-up helper 36% (reviews of money and permission
  code), routine helpers 4%.
- **A running conversation crossing programs would cost the most:** the other program rereads it all, without
  the cache, and the history doesn't carry across.

## Proposed shape

1. **Cost in each subscription's own terms.** Both subscriptions are paid either way. What a task costs is the
   share of its program's week it takes, read from colony's records of week percent against tokens by model.
   API dollars don't measure it. The aim: both weeks used close to fully by their resets, at the most points,
   with neither running out early. The person's own limits per program (R168) bound each week.
2. **Crossing only where context is rebuilt anyway.** Helpers (builds, reviews, research) and consultations start
   fresh whichever program runs them, so choosing the other program costs no extra handoff. A project's agent
   stays on its own program; moving a live conversation across is the waste the person named.
3. **Auto picks each helper tier across both programs.** It takes the best points per share of week among
   programs with room. A program whose week runs ahead of pace yields to the other. A Fable-style limit on one
   model counts as that model spent, as now.
4. **One way to hand a brief to the other program's helper.** Two projects each built their own by hand,
   and one asked colony for one. The brief and the report stay files in the project, and the helper
   works in its own worktree.
5. **Handoff judged by size.** A task worth handing off is large compared with what the helper must read to start
   (about 40% of a run goes on that). Small tasks stay with whoever holds the context. The guidance says so in a
   line, and a measured result adjusts it.
6. **Second opinions kept possible.** Helpers stop taking Codex near the end of its week, so a consultation can
   still get the other family's view.
7. **A model with its own small week goes where intelligence counts most** (the person's point, 2026-11-17; their
   words in R169.words.md). Fable is the best buy in dollars at almost every level, so pricing in dollars spreads it
   over everything, trivial work included. Priced by its own week, it is scarce:
   - **Who gets it first:** the roles at the top of the scale, consultants and the step-up helper. The step-up
     helper's Fable reviews of money and permission code found must-fix defects 9 times in 12.
   - **Main agents:** Fable for a hard stretch of work, if the week has room. Their everyday turns (tests, reads,
     notes) run on the next model down.
   - **Routine, chores and rote:** never Fable.
   - **Pacing:** colony can't read Fable's own percent until it is spent. It counts Fable's use itself, from the
     transcripts, against what one Fable week held: about $1,070 at API prices last time. Each later run-out sharpens
     that estimate.

## How it will be checked

- **Replay.** Last week's helper runs go through the new picker: which program each would have gone to, and
  where both weeks would have ended.
- **One live week**, where each of these must hold:
  - Neither week hits 100% before its reset, and both end above about 90%.
  - Builds handed to the other program need no more rework than same-program builds: review findings, redone
    work, retries.
  - The share spent before the first edit doesn't rise.
  - Fable lasts to its reset, and no consultation or step-up run finds it spent.
- **Rollback:** if handed-off builds come back worse, Auto keeps helpers on the agent's own program and the
  reading goes to the person.

## Decisions it turns on

- **Cost per task: share of week (default) or API dollars.** Not stale: subscriptions are this person's own case.
- **Where the line falls for a handoff:** the size rule above as a start, adjusted by what the live week shows.
- **What share of Codex's week the consultations keep.** Default: helpers stop at 90% of its week.

No research flag: the routing literature prices tokens in dollars, and the subscription case is measured here.

  roles leave; the live week shows that.
