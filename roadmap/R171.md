# R171 — History kept by section, with a one-line ledger

The person's idea, 2026-11-19 (their words: `R171.words.md`, private), widening a recommendation of mine (keep
project conversations past Claude Code's 30-day cleanup, from a project's report on another agent manager).

## Why

- **History is being lost.** Claude Code deletes a conversation 30 days after its last use (`cleanupPeriodDays`,
  unset here, so the default). On Nov 19 no Claude transcript older than Oct 20 was left on this machine. Three
  projects that are off had their newest conversation 16 to 17 days old, so they lose them, with their Chat history
  and resume, from about Dec 1. Codex keeps its sessions.
- **Old history has no shape.** A conversation that has run for weeks is one file of hundreds of MB, cut at each
  compaction. An agent that needs something from far back has its own compaction summary and nothing else.
- **Far-back items can't be placed in time.** Colony records when an item started and finished (`item_times`), but
  not when it was created, and shows none of it once the item is done.

## What the evidence says

A project's recall test on the person's own history (Oct 28-29, 45 questions): a plain agent searching the raw
history recalled 88%; a ledger compacted at each boundary, 65%; one compaction of the whole, 38%. So the sections
stay whole and searchable, and the one-line titles are an index over them, never a replacement. The test did not
measure a titled index; this item does, by its checks.

## Shape

- **Sections.** Each project's conversations (both programs) are cut where the program compacted them; a
  conversation's last span stays open until its next compaction. PROVIDER: Claude Code marks a boundary with a
  `compact_boundary` record and its summary; Codex with a `compacted` record.
- **A one-line title for each closed section,** written by the project's rote helper model on the project's own
  program (so no party sees what it hasn't), from the section's digest: the person's messages, the agent's replies,
  and the summary written at its end, capped. Titled by the watcher a few at a time.
- **Stored in colony's home, never in the project.** Each section's prose (the person's and the agent's words, and
  one line for each command run) is kept as text under colony's home, since several projects keep `.board/` in
  their own git. Before Claude Code's cleanup reaches a project transcript, colony keeps a compressed copy there too;
  the project's current conversation is kept fresh instead, so Chat and resume go on working.
- **The agent's way in, one command:** `colony history` (the ledger: dates, title, id), `colony history ID` (that
  section's summary and the person's words; `--raw` for all its prose), `colony history --search WORDS`, and
  `colony history --item R2` (the item's created, started and finished dates, the sections from its time, and the
  commits naming it). The agent is told where it needs it: the line colony prints after each compaction names it,
  and nothing is added to standing instructions.
- **Item dates.** Colony adds when each item was created (from the roadmap's git history, so it applies back to
  the start), and the board shows created · started · finished on each done item.

## Decisions

- Where the dates show: on the board and through `colony history --item`, not written into ROADMAP.md, whose
  finished items stay terse titles (`guides/roadmap.md`) and which only its lead edits. Mine.
- Titles from the section's digest rather than its raw text: a section runs to 200k tokens or more, its digest a
  few thousand. Mine.

## Checks

1. Tests on made-up transcripts of both programs: sections, prose, titles (a fake model), search, item view, and
   keeping (a current transcript touched, an idle one copied).
2. On this machine: every project's history built; the sections found equal the compactions counted; ten titles
   read against their sections.
3. A fresh agent, given only `colony history`, answers what happened in one of colony's early items and when;
   checked against git.
4. A project's conversation, left off since early November, is still there after Dec 1 (a wake).

## Results, 2026-11-19

1. **Tests:** 1,185 pass, the made-up transcripts of both programs among them.
2. **This machine:** 14 projects' histories built, 333 sections titled. Each Claude conversation has one section per
   compaction; where it has one more, colony closed the span itself when it kept a quiet conversation (one project's,
   before Claude Code's cleanup reaches it). Ten titles read against their sections across five projects: eight
   match what their sections did; the two on Codex were right too, but showed that the "summary" kept for a Codex
   section was the last message said, since Codex seals its own (fixed). Fixed along the way: one project's ledger
   was 309 of 365 lines one-shot Codex runs; a seat listed its folder's other conversations; items done before
   dates were kept showed the day colony first saw them as their finish; two titles began "Title:".
3. **A fresh agent** (Sonnet 5.5, medium), six questions with a key written first, two runs per arm, graded blind
   (Opus 5.5) 0-2 each. Three questions on early items git records well; two on talk that never reached git; one
   spread across many weeks (what happened to the machine). The history was frozen at the test's start so the
   agents couldn't find my own searches for the answers.

   | Arm | Score (of 24) | Git-recorded items (of 12) | Talk only (of 12) | Cost per run |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | History only | 23 | 11 | 12 | $0.15 |
   | Git only | 13 | 11 | 2 | $0.26 |
   | Both | 24 | 12 | 12 | $0.18 |

   Where the work was built and committed, git told the story as well as the history (the person's expectation).
   What was only said (a question answered, advice given, a machine problem diagnosed) only the history had. The
   one point the history lost was a decision git recorded and the history held only as a recommendation; with
   both, nothing was lost. The person's suggestion that an agent looking for something would do best with both is
   what the both arm measured. Limits: one project, whose commits are unusually full; six questions, chosen
   knowing which source held each answer, so they show that each source holds things the other lacks, not how
   often; one model.
4. Waits for Dec 2 (wake).
