# R158 `colony friction`: the friction on record in one list, run on demand

## Where it comes from

The person's idea, in three steps on Nov 11 (note n00c7d963 on gate gce677707, then two messages in Chat; their
words are kept privately, my reading is in roadmap/R146.md): friction reports read together for shared causes, in
the form of a command in the manner of `colony doctor`, ready but unused until someone runs it. They approved the
build and the name (gate g53db8b38).

A trial of the reading, done once by hand (roadmap/R146.md), showed what the command is for: read together, ten
reports that had been fixed one by one were one kind, and two faults in colony's own record came to light. The slow
and unreliable part was gathering the entries, which is what this does.

## Shape

- `colony friction [--since DATE]`: prints what is on record since a date (two weeks back unless said), a line each
  with its date, project and reference. In colony's own project it covers every project; in any other project, that
  project's own entries.
- It gathers, with no model run and keeping nothing:
  - dev reports to colony, each with colony's answer if one is found;
  - the person's corrections (matched plainly), and their words that may be one (matched by phrase, said as such);
  - questions put to the person again in a new form;
  - agents' own statements of a mistake, from the replies the board keeps (both programs);
  - work redone, from the plan and the commits.
- It ends with what the list cannot show: above all, friction no one noticed, which is found only by counting.
- The reading is the caller's: group by cause, judge colony's or inherent, report to the person. Nothing is built
  from a report by itself; bugs are still fixed as they arrive.
- No words in any other project's context. Colony's own instructions (the part of CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md read
  only by an agent working on colony) say to run it once a week and keep a weekly wake for it: the person's order,
  given while it was being built.

## The person's further idea, and what I decided

A project might look over its own friction when a milestone finishes, to see whether something recurs that deserves
a rule; if that would not pay, it stays off. They left the shape to me. Decided: the command works in any project
for its own entries, so the look is possible, and nothing prompts it. A prompt at a milestone is a standing request
at a moment, and the nearest thing already built (lessons proposed at a milestone) has had two proposals and none
adopted. If a look by hand shows it pays, the prompt can be proposed then.

A first look by hand, after the build: one project's own list for the period shows a recurrence (four of its six
own-mistake entries are one kind), so the look can pay. That project was told the command exists and invited to try
it at its next finished milestone and say whether it showed anything a rule would fix. One look, not a duty.

The project's answer, the same day (mail mfe6e31a3): it tried the look, and it showed that one kind. The act was
already named by a line in the project's own list of lessons, and its two newest cases happened with that line in
place, so knowing the line had not stopped it. It set itself a sharper rule, in its own memory: one that makes a
missing read show in the sentence. Nothing for colony to build.

Decided after it: still no prompt. One look paid, but it was not an independent one (my invitation had named the
recurrence), and a first look reads a backlog that later looks will not have. Whether the project's rule holds
shows at its next look; that, and whether a second look finds anything, is the evidence a prompt would need. The
finding that a known line did not prevent the act goes into the weekly reading (the same kind is on record in
three projects).

2. Run over Nov 1 to 11 on the real records: it lists the 18 dev reports the trial used, the reply the count of own
   mistakes was checked against, and no copies from the helper seat.
3. The full suite passes before the board restarts.

## Restraint, removal and a cleaning reading (the person's direction, Nov 11, after the build)

Their words are kept privately; my reading:

- A reading must not incite over-building in colony. A build is proposed from one only where its result is likely or
  obvious, and taking something out is weighed as readily as adding.
- What is built and does not show such a result is removed. Applied to this command first: on Dec 9, the fourth
  reading, the report says what the readings led to; if nothing of that kind, the recommendation is to remove the
  weekly run and the command.
- Their question, whether it should also clean, and their point that smarter models will make parts unnecessary and
  nothing in colony addresses that. My answer, theirs to strike: every fourth reading is a cleaning reading. What was
  used in the last four weeks (commands run, guides opened, refusals by the shell check, rules that applied) is
  counted from transcripts and records by a throwaway script, and what went unused is proposed for removal. When the
  model behind the agents changes, the next cleaning reading counts how often each guard still fires: a guard that is
  code shows this by itself (its refusals are in the transcripts), words do not, and for those the test is a trial
  removal watched in live use. No new command or store for any of this.
- What colony has today that bears on growth: the always-on block has a tested ceiling, and the cleanup check
  (R139) says when shrinking a text pays for itself. Neither asks whether a part is still needed.

The person's refinement of the cleaning reading, the same day (their words kept privately; my reading). They took
the cleaning reading up and corrected its test:

- A count of firings is not enough. For what fired, the reading looks at where it fired and judges whether it helped
  there or got in the way.
- Not firing is no reason to cut. A part that does not fire weighs nothing, and it may still serve a weaker model
  (the cheaper helper tiers run one) or the rare condition it was made for.
- So what a cleaning reading proposes for removal is what gets in the way where it fires, and what weighs on every
  turn for no result (words every agent reads, whether or not they ever apply; for those, the recorded reason for
  the line is read first). My earlier form, proposing whatever went unused, is withdrawn.

Where all of this belongs, the person's next message the same day (my reading): these are developer functions,
used by the agent working on colony itself for the purpose of developing colony further. They are not a part of
using colony that a project or another user is promised. So the command leaves the README's list of everyday
commands for a line under developing colony, its help line says so, and no project is prompted to run it (as
before). It still answers in any project for that project's own entries, silently; the one invited look stands as
a trial, and a prompt at a milestone is not planned.

The standing rule (u76cca1a7, the form that stands; three earlier forms the same day were retired) and colony's
own instructions carry this, and point to the test below.

## The cleaning test, as it stands (every fourth reading; first on Dec 9)

The person shaped this in three messages on Nov 11 (their words kept privately); this is my reading of where it
stands, and the one place the rule and colony's instructions point to.

1. Count, over the four weeks, for each part that can fire (the shell check's refusals, colony's notices, standing
   rules, commands, guides opened): the chances it had, the times it fired, and of those the times it helped and the
   times it got in the way. Where there are many firings, a sample is read where each happened. A throwaway script
   over transcripts and records; no new command or store.
2. Judge by share, not by number: helped out of fired, and fired out of chances. One of one is not one of a
   thousand, whichever fires more often. (I read the person's "one of one against one of a thousand" as the share
   of firings that helped; it could also mean firings out of chances. Both are counted, so neither reading is lost.)
3. Silence is never by itself a reason to cut. A part that did not fire may serve a weaker model, or the rare
   condition it was made for.
4. Weight is judged with worth, not alone. Words every agent reads on every turn weigh whether or not they apply.
   Even so, a guard rail understood to be fundamentally helpful in the rare case it is needed is not judged by a
   quiet period. What is weighed together: the weight carried, how rarely it is needed, and how much it helps when it
   is. Only a part that weighs, almost never applies, and helps little when it does is a candidate.
5. What gets in the way where it fires is a candidate whatever its weight.
6. A candidate is a proposal to the person, with its counts and its recorded reason. Nothing is removed by a reading.

The Dec 9 reading also carries one count from elsewhere: the research flag kept on the library (roadmap/R159.md,
check 5): flagged items since Nov 11, whether research happened at their start, whether it changed anything.
