# R144 A fresh context at each new item

The person, 2026-11-02: "Should we also force a /compact for colony projects before starting a new roadmap item?"

My reading: the item boundary is the natural seam. What the finished item read and debugged is no longer needed, and
since R143 the next item's brief is its details file and its related items', made for a fresh start. Today colony
refreshes a context only at 60% of the window (context.SOFT), which on a 1M window is 600k, so leads carry 300k+
from item to item: this conversation was 342k on Nov 2, and every step re-reads all of it at 0.1x.

Why it should pay: a refresh costs about one read of the context plus a short summary (about 80k weighted at 342k),
and then saves (old - new context) x 0.1 on every step after: break-even in about three steps, and an item runs
dozens to hundreds. Evidence so far: the long-context test's round 1 (design/ctxcut/) cut helpers at about 250k and
used 26-56% fewer tokens with quality not worse in the two tasks judged; no verdict yet, and that was helpers on
one task, not a lead across items, whose continuity with the person matters more.

Shape to test: when an item is marked done and the context is over a floor (about 150k), colony refreshes it at the
lead's next idle moment through its existing refresh (carry-over and the last exchanges kept), so the next item
starts from its brief. Not a forced /compact mid-turn: the agent can't compact itself, and carrying on across items
often happens within one turn.

Check: a blind test after the weekly reset (the person's rule ue9f30d9a holds building until then): leads replayed
across a real item boundary, with and without the refresh; measure the next item's tokens, its quality (tests,
judge) and continuity (does it still act on what the person said in the item before). Ships only if the next item
is no worse and the person's recent direction carries over. Decide with the long-context test's verdict, since the
two share a mechanism (the autocompact window) and may make each other unnecessary.

Order: after the restart, the long-context test's cheap analysis on saved runs (judge the picking and import-fix tasks, the full check for lost details), its results to the person, then this shape.

The person, 2026-11-02, on the shape's part 2: "it's not common for the user to dump huge swaths of text as much as it
will be for attached documents attached pictures or maybe even links to look at long indexes". So the reference part
is mostly an index, not copies: attached files and pictures are already on disk (Chat's attachments), so compaction
loses only that they exist and what they were for; links can be fetched again, but pages change and fetching costs, so
what a fetch returned is saved when it was long; a long paste is saved verbatim (the rarer case). A script builds the
index from the transcript (attachment paths, image reads, fetched URLs and their results), and the carry-over adds
each one's purpose in a few words. The restored context always shows the index; the agent re-opens a document,
re-looks at a picture or re-reads a saved page only when the work needs it.

The person, 2026-11-02, on the "wait for a lull" rule: "I don't know if actual time is a good framing for whether or
not we are done with an item. Sometimes I will go hours without getting back to the conversation, but the
conversation is still the same topic." Dropped: quiet time doesn't say a topic is done. Instead a compaction never
needs the topic closed: the carry-over names the open topic, and its exchanges since it began are saved verbatim to
a reference file in the index, so the agent can reread the discussion itself when the person comes back. With that, a
long pause is the cheapest moment for a large conversation: a lead's cache lapses after an hour, so their next message
would rewrite the whole context at 2x (342k: about 680k weighted); compacting a little before the hour costs one cached
read and a short summary (about 140k), and every step after re-reads the small context.

roadmap items kept and tagged to each roadmap item. Or it knows it was run after the relevant roadmaps are complete and
deletes it. Just a thought - maybe theres a smarter way". My proposal, from that: the conversation's reference list is

My reading: yes, and it replaces guessing seams from time: the agent is the one that sees a topic close. Shape: a
command, not a marker in the reply (a command is exact; text has to be parsed): `colony newtopic "what the last topic
settled, in a line"`. Colony then files the closed topic's exchanges verbatim as a reference (into the item it was
about, or the list), and refreshes at the agent's next idle moment, so the new topic starts in a small context with
its first exchange kept. It counts only above a floor (about 150k); below, nothing happens. The rule is one line in
the always-loaded guidance, and colony's prompt hook adds it as a reminder when the context first passes x (about
150k) and again at y (about 300k), never per turn: guidance at its moment, as R140 found works, and merely-available
guidance goes unused (R32's evidence). The closing line is the structure the person means: each topic leaves a
settled line where its item is. Seams then: an item done (colony sees it), a new topic (the agent says so), and the
size backstop for a conversation that never closes.
