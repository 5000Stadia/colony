# R154 What the person hears: an item's shape, not each nail

Agreed 2026-11-10 (gate g0b5df7c2: yes to both, the draft and the fold) and built. Their words: `roadmap/R154.words.md` (git-ignored).

## My reading (2026-11-09)

The person asked whether a project's chat tells them the shape of a roadmap item as it forms, then asked for a
draft of guidance. What they meant, as I read it:
- The person is to the agent as an architect to a builder (their pictures: a project manager with a say in the
  design; a foreman who isn't told each nail). They want the structure: what a piece is, where it sits among the
  others, and what needs weighing (options, a shape that could go more than one way, planning).
- How it was built stays out, unless it is the thing to weigh.
- A rule that is followed by writing more is a failure: the result must be shorter and clearer, not a longer form.

## What was seen

One project's chat over 24 hours, read by me:
- 291 messages: 11 ended a turn, 280 came mid-work. Of the 280, 216 were under 220 characters (running commentary
  between steps) and 13 were reports of 600 or more.
- A new item was started on the agent's own judgement with its why-now and decisions in the item's notes, not in
  chat. Five and a half hours and four questions later the person said it wasn't wanted yet.
- The person said they had opened none of the links it produced, for keeping up with its chat.
- What it did well: plain openings for some items, its own calls and slips named, questions with a recommendation
  and a default.

Colony's part: the idle wake starts the next item with no word to the person asked for; Chat shows every line said
between steps in full; nothing in the deployed block says what the person should hear.

## The draft (as tested: in the deployed block; where it went is under What was built)

    - **What the person hears.** They are the architect, you the builder: they weigh the design; the nails are yours.
      Chat carries only:
      - an item starting, in five short lines at most: what it is; why now; what it rests on or changes among the other
        items; what done looks like; what it will ask of them;
      - something to weigh (real options, a finding that moves the plan): the options, a line each, and yours, before
        you build on one;
      - an item done, in three lines: what stands now, and how to see it.
      The rest (how it was built, what runs meanwhile, tests, commits, files) stays in the item's notes. Name things by
      what they are, not by number.
    - Keep approved work queued, so a pending answer or a running helper never leaves you idle.

## The test (2026-11-09)

Three real moments from that project (an item starting, twice; research in with decisions formed), each written
once per version by Fable 5.2 from a clean start, given the deployed block, the roadmap and the item's notes as they
stood. Words in the message:

| version | start 1 | start 2 | decisions | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| block as deployed | 277 | 312 | 377 | 322 |
| first draft (three moments named, no sizes) | 353 | 355 | 366 | 358 |
| one sentence (builder to architect, never each nail) | 319 | 360 | 352 | 344 |
| the draft above (sizes given) | 186 | 210 | 365 | 254 |
| one sentence with "five short lines or fewer" | 153 | 145 | 207 | 168 |

- Naming what to say without a size made messages longer: the person's warning, measured.
- With sizes, the two openings fell about a third and came out as labelled lines: what, why now, what it rests
  on, done, what it will ask.
- The one sentence with a size was shortest but dropped why-now and where the item sits, and went back to what the
  helpers were doing and to bare item numbers.
- The decisions message stayed about 365 words in every full version: four questions and two costs take the room.
  A clause telling it not to repeat questions already on the board changed nothing (360), so it is left out.

Limits: one write per cell, read by me, not judged blind. A clean start can't show the commentary between steps,
which is most of the volume. The real check is the draft live on that project for a day: messages and words per
day, whether each new item opens with its shape, and whether the person opens what is shown.

## The other half: Chat folds the commentary (the proposal as it was put)

Guidance against narrating fights the programs' own habit, and a line between steps is useful while work is live.
So: in Chat, a short line said between steps folds into the line that already holds the work around it ("Ran 6
commands"), opened with a tap; the newest stays visible while the turn is live; a long mid-work message (a report)
and the turn's last message stay open. On the day read above, that folds 216 of 291 messages. Reading aloud
follows what is open.

## Their answer, and what was built (2026-11-10)

Yes to both. Their addition, as I read it: what an agent says between steps is also its own working notes, and it
steers the agent, so the guidance must not silence it. It should shape it: short, focused lines that serve the agent
mid-work and read well to the person.

Built:
- **Where the guidance lives.** Not in the always-on block: that has a tested ceiling of 1,000 words (R140:
  guidance arrives at its moment) and six to spare, and the draft is some 150. So it rides the moments that already
  point to guides: the roadmap guide's Starting an item (the five lines, architect and builder, what stays in the
  notes, and the working note between steps) and Finishing an item (three lines), and the asking guide (something
  to weigh). The idle wake, which is where an item starts unasked, now says to tell the person its shape as it
  starts. The always-on block is unchanged.
- **A working note** (board.a_note): one line of 280 characters or fewer that a step follows in its turn. A longer
  or many-line message mid-work is a report; a turn's last words are its reply. Both stay as they were.
- **Chat** folds a note into the line that holds the work around it ("Ran 6 commands · 2 notes"), opened with a tap,
  the notes in their place among the steps. The newest line of a turn at work has nothing after it yet, so it shows
  until more work comes.
- **Reading aloud** follows: when a step follows a note, the feeder marks it folded in the heard record, so it
  leaves the line unheard, and a reply tapped or played again from History leaves its notes out. This was my
  addition (the person listens more than they read, and a fold that still read every line aloud would undo what
  they agreed to); it is theirs to reverse.

How it is checked: the tests (a turn with notes, a report and more work, in Chat and in the line); a look at a live
project's Chat; then a day of it on the project it was drawn from: messages and words per day, whether each new
item opens with its shape, and whether the person opens what is shown.
