# R150 Status reports

## The request (2026-11-08)

The person asked for status reports built into colony, in place of one of the steward's last uses: a button that
has a fresh agent write an overview of every open project and of what needs them, with one place to answer it all;
off by default, with an optional schedule about as flexible as a desktop task scheduler's (an hourly interval, a
time of day, days of the week); and a feed of earlier reports. The prompt it serves is the orientation they had
asked the lead for that day: what each decision is, whether there is an obvious choice, the options' pros and
cons, a recommendation, and everything numbered so they can answer briefly. Their words are in R150.words.md, kept on this machine, out of the public repository.

## My reading

- Context: that day the person asked the lead for an orientation after falling behind. It took a long turn of
  gathering every project's gates, asks and state by hand. They want that on a button, without a project's agent
  spending its context on it, and without the steward.
- The report is written by a fresh model call, not a standing agent. Colony gathers the facts itself: each project's
  state, what it is at, what moved lately, what its agent last told the person, and every moment waiting on them, by
  the one definition (`waiting_items`). The writer gets only that and writes the orientation. It has no tools and no
  files, so it can't act and has nothing to wander into.
- One place to answer: every numbered item carries its live answer underneath, the same controls Needs you
  uses (the options as taps, a box for anything else). A report is a snapshot, but its answers are live: an item
  answered since shows the answer.
- What colony gathers is complete by construction. An item the writer leaves out is still listed, under its own
  question. A reference the writer invents is dropped.
- Off by default. The schedule is one of: every N hours from a start time, or once a day at a time, either on the days
  ticked (weekdays at noon, or every four hours). A slot missed while the board was down runs once if the board is
  back within the hour, and never for slots before the schedule was set.
- The feed: newest first, with earlier reports folded below it. That isn't chat-like: reports are read from the top,
  and a long report at the bottom of a chat would land the person at its end.
- The steward: this takes over one of its last reasons to stay. Whether it goes is the person's call, once they've
  used this (an older open item, R125).

## Check

- Tests, on a test board:
  - The slots: weekdays at noon, every 4 hours from 8:00, and the ticked days.
  - Slots: nothing for slots before the schedule was set, one report per slot, a late board fires within the grace
    and not after, and Off never fires.
  - Gathering: every moment across the projects gets a reference, a paused project's are counted, and the writer's
    output parses (plain JSON, fenced JSON, or broken, which falls back to the raw questions).
  - The page:
    - each item shows the writer's analysis with its live controls;
    - an item the writer skipped is still there, and an invented reference is gone;
    - an answered gate shows its answer;
    - newest first, with earlier reports folded;
    - the button starts one report, and a second press while it writes starts none;
    - the schedule form saves, and its summary reads right.
- Live: a real report from the button on the person's board, read against the lead's hand-written orientation of
  Nov 8 (does it cover every decision; are the recommendations sound), checked at phone width, with its cost noted.

## Listen on each report (2026-11-09)

Asked in chat on Nov 9 to hear each status report read aloud (their words in R150.words.md). My reading: each report,
the newest and the folded ones alike, gets its own play button, heard in parts as the board already plays a document.

- Built: a Listen button on every report. `/reports/audio?r=ID` turns the report into a document for the board's
  player: the overview with how many items wait, then each item numbered (its project and kind, situation, options
  with pros and cons, recommendation), then each project's state and status. Each is a section, so ⏭/⏮ and the lock
  screen step through them, and the bar's section list jumps to one. It is read in colony's own voice.
- An item answered since the report says "No longer waiting on you" and its options aren't read. The writer's own
  heading marks are flattened, so they can't break an item into extra sections.
- Checked: unit test (sections in order, answered item, route and 404). Live headless check at 390px dark and desktop
  light: Listen on the newest and on a folded report (which stays folded) loads all 30 sections, ⏭ goes from the
  overview to item 1, no page errors, no sideways scroll.

## Skipped clips, and the bar's count (2026-11-09)

Reported in chat on Nov 9 (their words in R150.words.md): after skipping past several readings quickly, the one
landed on took long to start, and they suspected the board was still making each skipped clip in turn. They asked
that a skipped clip leave the line, that the one being listened to be made first and the one after it next, and
that going back to a skipped one simply makes it the one listened to. Then, on the bar's "3 of 7": it wasn't clear
what it counted, and it should show only how many come after the one playing.

My reading: a fault in every reading (replies and documents alike), and a small change to the bar.

- **The fault, confirmed.** The page dropped its ask on a skip, but the board went on making every clip it had been
  asked for, one at a time, in the order asked. Measured on the newest report with a voice that had nothing kept:
  after seven skips 350 ms apart, the section landed on began 24.6 s later.
- **Fixed.**
  - The voice has an ordered line (`voice._turn`). The clip being listened to comes before one made ahead; of those
    listened to, the newest ask first; those made ahead, in the order asked.
  - An asker that has left gives up its place: while it waits, the board checks whether the page closed the
    connection, and makes nothing for it. The clip under way when the skip came is finished and kept.
  - The page says which asks are made ahead (`pre=1`), and on any move (⏭, ⏮, a section, another reply) lets go
    the asks the new place doesn't want. Before, a skip within a chat reply kept them.
- **Checked.** A unit test of the order and of a closed connection being seen. Live, the same seven skips: 3.1 s on
  the board's own address and 3.1 s through its Tailscale address, with the seven skipped clips never made.
- **The bar** now reads "Colony · 3 more" (the replies that come after the one being read, in a line run or a
  History run, counting any reading ⏮ went back from), and "last one" on the last.
