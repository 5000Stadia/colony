# R165 — Backup built in

Where colony keeps a nightly copy of itself is a setting, and colony makes the copy.

## Where it comes from

The person asked for it on 2026-11-15 (their words and my reading: roadmap/R165.words.md, private). Two days before,
after a drive fault, the copy had been set up by hand outside colony: a shell script and a systemd timer calling
`colony migrate --pack` (R127) onto a spare drive. Doing it by hand showed what the built-in one has to get right:

- The copy skipped every night for two days because the drive was not mounted where the script looked. A drive is
  known by its own id and found wherever it is mounted, not by a path.
- A machine can hold another drive that must not be written to. Nothing is ever written to a drive other than the
  one chosen: with that drive absent, no copy is made and the person is told, once.
- A drive attached but not mounted is mounted the way the signed-in person would (udisks), never with sudo.

## What it is

- **The setting**: `backup`, a folder. Empty means no copy is kept; colony chooses none for the person (rule
  u384ea215). Settings → Backup shows it, the last copy made (when, how large), or why the last one was not made,
  and has Copy now. `colony settings backup DIR` sets it; `colony backup` says where things stand, `--now` makes one.
- **Choosing a folder**: it must be outside what the copy takes (the projects, colony's own records). On another
  drive, that drive's id and the folder's place on it are kept (Linux, by `findmnt`); on the same drive as colony's
  records it is allowed (a synced folder is a fair place) and Settings says plainly that it does not survive that
  drive failing.
- **The copy**: the archive `colony migrate --pack` makes (every project, colony's records, the agents'
  conversations; read back whole before it is kept), restored with `migrate --unpack`. The newest four are kept;
  when the drive is short of room the oldest go first, never the only one.
- **When**: the watcher starts it, in a process of its own at low priority, at the first pass at or after 3:30 AM
  each day, so a machine that was off makes it when it is next on. One at a time.
- **When it is not made**: a notice at the top of the front page says why, once for each reason (and again after a
  week if it still stands), not every night.

## How it is checked

- Tests: a folder inside a project is refused; a folder chosen on another filesystem is not written to when that
  filesystem is gone; a copy is due once a night and not twice; the oldest archives go, the newest four stay; a
  failure is told once.
- Live: the setting pointed at the spare drive in use since Nov 15, Copy now pressed, the archive listed and read
  back, and the hand-made script and timer retired.

- Tests: tests/test_backup.py, five, each of the points above; the whole suite passes (1,166).
- Live: the setting points at the spare drive's own folder, found by the drive's id. Copy now on the board started a
  copy in its own process: 28.9 GB in 369 seconds, read back whole, recorded as the last copy, two archives kept
  beside the one made by hand an hour earlier. The hand-made script, service and timer are removed.
- Seen (2026-11-16): the watcher started the night's copy by itself at 3:30 AM: 28.9 GB in 321 seconds, three kept.
- Not yet seen: the drive found at its new mount point after a restart.
