# R160 The Inbox: what needs the person, one item at a time, in an order that is built in; and no choice made by omission

## Where it comes from

The person's idea, in six messages in Chat on Nov 11 (their words kept privately in roadmap/R160.words.md; this is
the lead's reading). What needs them holds projects up, and three shapes built for it (the pop-ups, the Needs you
list, the status report) had not made them answer it well. Their proposal: a section they call Inbox, where one
item fills the page with everything needed to judge it, they answer, and the next appears; an order that is built
in rather than chosen; deadlines that raise an item; a way to put an item at the bottom of the stack; and items
that show only from a date. The lead laid out a shape and four points where it differed; they approved all of it
and asked that the second-step parts be built too.

In the same conversation they said a choice taken because they did nothing is not a precedent they want, and
that it should be off unless they say otherwise. That is built here as well.

## What was counted first (all projects, the 30 days to Nov 11, from the board's records)

- Decisions with answers to tap were answered: 97 of 157 by the person, half within 1.8 hours, 42 within the hour,
  7 after more than a day. 34 were withdrawn by their agents.
- What sat was work to look at: of 10 items waiting on running projects, 6 were built work wanting an OK, five of
  them waiting 31 to 37 hours. The list showed a sign-off's demo address as plain text and its pictures as a path
  on the machine.
- 14 defaults had come to stand because the decision was skipped (18 since the start). Seven kept things as they
  were (stay parked, wait, not now); seven settled something about the work. 105 of the 157 decisions carried a
  default, so their own agents had judged most of them skippable.
- Where standing defaults came from: R80 (Oct 8), modelled on another project's daily page and agreed in a short
  message together with four other things.

## Shape

- **The Inbox** (`/inbox`, colony/inbox.py): what the Needs you chip opens. One card: the project, the kind of
  thing, how long it has waited, its deadline, the item it belongs to, what the project put in its showcase for
  that item, then the moment itself with its answers open. After an answer the page returns to the stack and the
  next card is on top. "See all" opens the Needs you list, which leads back.
- **One definition.** The stack is `board.waiting_items` as moments, so the count, the list, the pop-ups, the
  status report and the Inbox agree. Sign-offs with no question beside them come one a card.
- **The order, built in.** An item with a deadline leads once inside its window (the day before, unless its agent
  said longer), the nearest deadline first; then the rest, the longest-waiting first. No choice of sort, no urgent
  flag: the person's own example of urgency was a deadline, and a flag any agent can set is set by all.
- **Not now** orders an item as if it had just arrived. A deadline item put off is back on top within the hour
  (sooner as the deadline nears), so nothing is missed at the bottom.
- **Later** takes an item off every list until the day the person picks (tomorrow, or a date), then it returns by
  itself where its age puts it. Its agent hears quietly. What is set aside is listed under the card, each with
  Bring back. This stands in for colony guessing that one answer settles another item: the board cannot read
  meaning, the agent that receives an answer can withdraw its own questions, and across projects the person says it
  in one tap.
- **Dates on a decision**: `colony gate ... --by WHEN [--rise 7d]` for a deadline the world sets, `--from DAY`
  for a question that cannot be acted on before a day (off the list until then). Two sentences in
  guides/asking.md; nothing in the always-on block. Nothing happens by itself when a deadline passes.
- **How to check a sign-off** is drawn as a showcase entry's Try is: addresses and the project's paths are links,
  and the pictures it names (a file, or a folder's first six) show in place. In the list as well as on the card.
- **No choice by omission.** A default no longer stands when a decision is skipped: it is shown as the agent's
  default, one tap to take, and the decision waits. guides/asking.md no longer offers a default; it says a choice
  with an obvious best is the agent's to take and say, and that what is asked waits. `colony gate --default` still
  works and says the same back, with how to withdraw the gate if one answer is plainly best. The one thing that
  still lapses when skipped is a request for the person's verdict on finished work, which closes a question and
  decides nothing.

## Not built, and why

- Acting for the person at a real deadline. Their condition for it (a literal deadline in the world, critical to
  the vision, plainly better than missing it, and declared acceptable by them) is recorded; no such item exists
  today, so it is a proposal for when one does.
- A status report for each card (their thought in the sixth message, which they doubted themselves). The report's
  writer adds pros and cons for each option and a second recommendation; it costs a model run for each item, and
  the reports have been run six times in all, last on Nov 8. Proposed only if cards turn out to say too little.
- Items posted by business systems (a tax date). The date flags are what such an item would use.

## How it is checked

1. Unit cases for each part of the shape: one card and the next after an answer over HTTP; the order across
   projects; Not now; Later, its return, Bring back, a past day refused; a deadline inside and outside its window,
   after Not now, and once passed; a start day; sign-offs one a card with links and pictures; a skipped default
   never standing while a default taken by the person does.
2. The live page built from the real records after the restart, and seen at phone width.
3. The suite passes before the board restarts.
4. On Nov 25, two weeks on, counted from the records: how long sign-offs wait (31 to 37 hours before), how often
   Not now and Later are used, how many decisions agents open and how many carry a default (105 of 157 before),
   and whether work stalled on an unanswered decision that would once have stood. If the Inbox shows no clear
   result, removing it is proposed.

## What was built, and the check's result

- colony/inbox.py (the stack, the card, Not now, Later, the page); in colony/board.py the routes, the chip, dates on
  a gate, `check_html`, the unfolded moment, set-aside items left out of `waiting_items`, and `stand_defaults` kept
  for verdict requests alone; `--by`, `--rise` and `--from` in colony/cli.py; the status report's brief and wording.
- Checks 1 and 3: five new test cases in tests/test_board.py (`InboxTest`) and one rewritten for defaults; two
  cases that tested how a default came to stand were removed with the behaviour, on the person's decision. The suite
  passes (1,142).
- Check 2: on the real records the stack held 10 cards, the oldest a sign-off waiting 38 hours, with its demo
  address a link and the project's shown stories as buttons; a sign-off naming a folder of pictures showed six of
  them. Seen at phone width by a headless render, not on the person's phone.
- Known and left: the chip counts moments (7) where the Inbox counts cards (10), because sign-offs come one a
  card. A console choice or a completed version has no time of its own on record, so it counts as waiting from
  when the Inbox first saw it.
