# R146 — Knowing whether colony's changes helped

The person, 2026-11-02: "One thing I'm worried about is between compaction and this type of research planning we are basing our evidence that it's a good direction on some good ideas I think with each idea there is a slight risk of us being wrong and never knowing it. In my opinion, any further testing is expensive. But as time goes on, we will never really know if we built our self into better quality or worse quality." Then, to save Anthropic tokens: "inform colony-codex of this idea in shape and encourage it to collaborate with the colony project to consider the best collaborative shape for colony". Then: "Yes all building after friday".

My reading: each change (topic closing, Q Branch, guidance edits) is checked once, on its own small evidence, when it ships; nothing checks the sum afterward. The person wants to know over months whether colony is making the work better or worse, without paying for repeated controlled tests. The vision's "wiser with every project" now says so.

The shape, combined from mine and colony-codex's (m6d69a1d1, design only, Codex read none of the other projects' history):
- A warning system, not a quality score. Three views kept apart, with no composite: outcomes (each project's acceptance checks, the person's verdicts where given), friction (corrections, repairs, reopened items, failed checks), cost (actual usage, abandoned work included). Unfinished items count, not only those marked [x]. "Too little evidence" said plainly; no green for a quiet week.
- Labels with evidence: explicit corrections and reopenings found by code first; a small cheap Claude batch labels ambiguous cases from short excerpts and the diff (missed requirement, defect or regression, changed intent, unknown), with a random sample of apparently clean items to find misses.
- A frozen ruler: the rubric, packet and resolved model recorded with each label; old labels never relabelled; a labeller change rereads saved anchor cases first and marks a break if judgements move.
- Exposure marked: which conversations actually ran topic closing, which items actually got a brief, and their versions, not only ship dates.
- Baseline: the mechanical signals from history by script (no model tokens), coverage shown; model labels only on a small sample.
- No hold-back trials yet; if attribution matters later, one bounded experiment on one class of work, the person approving first. A safety fix, an instruction or warranted research is never withheld.
- Board: a collapsed section in Measures (Outcomes / Repairs and corrections / Cost), each concerning point opening its case; a notice only for a real, supported concern; no correlation turned into a rule automatically.
- Cost: weekly totals by script; a labelling batch (about 15-30k cheap tokens, a planning estimate) only when new cases arrived; actual cost recorded afterward.
- It also finds the later rework Q Branch's learn step can't see at [x] (R131).

The weakest point (colony-codex's): silent mediocrity. Fewer corrections can mean less inspection; less rework, less ambitious work. It catches getting worse; it can't prove getting better. Open for the person: their own verdict at each milestone (through the board's existing "Ready for your OK" sign-off), occasional independent judging of finished work, or a warning system only.

Check before calling it done: on colony's own history, the reading reproduces cases already known (R25's rework cases, R144's pilot corrections) with their evidence, and a labeller swap on the anchor cases is caught as a break.

## Built

2026-11-06, after the weekly reset (436fca6, merged 7c5d7e0, deployed). `colony/warning_signs.py` reads every project's records without changing them. It keeps outcomes, friction and cost apart, each signal with its evidence, and records which mechanisms each item ran under, listing what the records can't show. A folded "Warning signs" section sits under Measures, and an Overview notice appears only for a concern that passes the thresholds: 6 earlier weeks with evidence, at least 4 signals, and a rate at least 2x the baseline's and 0.25 higher. The reading refreshes in the background every few hours. The cheap labelling batch runs at most once a day, only for new ambiguous cases. Changes of the person's intent are shown, not counted as friction. The baseline output stays in the board's home (`warnings/`), never in the repository.

Checked: 22 of 28 projects have usable records (44 weeks with evidence, 104 weeks of cost). It reproduced 10 of the 14 known cases with their evidence: the R25 rework cases traced, and three of R144's pilot corrections. It missed four:
- two where the later item names neither the original nor any rework word;
- one only partly found;
- R144's chat fix, typed straight into a console, which isn't read.

A labeller swap on the 8 anchors (a fake labeller that judged differently) was caught as a break; the same labeller again was not. A real batch of 18 cases on Haiku cost $0.004. No concern is raised yet, since no project has 6 earlier weeks for a comparison. Known limit: the labeller once read R144's pilot corrections as defects where they were changes of intent, so the rubric wants a look at its first revision, which the frozen ruler will record as a break. Its blind spot (it can't show getting better) is with the person (gate g259f14eb).

## The blind spot, decided (2026-11-08, gate g259f14eb)

The person skipped the question, so its default stood: their verdict at milestones. My reading: that's their answer as
the default, not a choice they argued for, so watch whether the questions get answered or skipped, and bring it back

Built (2c3fc4b): when a milestone newly finishes, colony asks once, per shared plan: "<project> finished <M>, <title>.
How did this turn out, compared with how this kind of work usually goes?", with Better, About the same or Worse, and
words if they like. The wording never changes, so the answers compare over months. Their answer reaches the project's
agent as their words. A skipped one stands as "No verdict" after a day on their page, quietly, and counts for nothing.
The warning view shows each week's verdicts beside its other outcomes. Milestones finished before the first look
aren't asked.

Independent judging (option B) is held in reserve, not built. I run it only when it would decide something: when the
person's verdicts and the warning signs disagree, either way round, or before a big change to the guidance every agent
follows.
