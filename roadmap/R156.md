# R156 An audit of colony as a whole: a short report, not a plan

Asked by the person on 2026-11-11 (their words: `R156.words.md`, kept on this machine).

## My reading

Colony has grown by many small changes, each reasonable alone. The person asks whether the sum is still an
efficient, elegant system: which parts make it effective, which get in the way, and whether any of its
architecture should be approached differently. They want a clean, short report of colony's own assessment. It
proposes; it builds and removes nothing.

## How it is made

- Counts from records already on this machine, by script, no model run: what each agent actually uses (commands,
  guides), what colony says to an agent and how often, how the person's decisions were answered, the size of each
  part.
- Two fresh readers who are given the code and the texts, not the lead's view: one on the architecture, one on
  everything colony puts in an agent's context.
- The lead's reading, with each difference from the fresh readers named.

## How it will be checked

- Every "used" or "unused" carries a count and where it came from.
- Every "gets in the way" names what was seen (a count, a project's report), not a taste.
- Where the lead and a fresh reader differ, the report says so.
- The report is short: one page and a table.
- Nothing else in colony changes because of it.

## The report (2026-11-11)

**In one line:** colony's core is sound and well used; what weighs on it is one overgrown file, instructions
said in several places, and a pace of adding that outruns checking what pays.

### What it is now

| | |
|---|---|
| Age | 7 weeks (first commit Sep 24), 1,325 commits |
| Code | 42,434 lines in 68 modules; 27,021 lines of tests (1,185 tests, 79 s) |
| Roadmap | 140 items done, 7 in progress, 3 not started |
| Commands | 60; about 30 were run ten times or more by agents in the last two weeks |
| Always in an agent's context | about 1,000 to 1,200 words, plus 250 to 350 at a session's start; nothing on an ordinary prompt |

Sources: the repository; a script over every Claude Code session on this machine for twelve weeks (8,491
sessions, 251,441 steps; Codex's sessions not counted); every project's decision records; two fresh readers.

### What makes it effective

1. **Plain files, consoles that outlive a restart, and one seam between the two programs.** The fresh reader
   would keep all three exactly as they are, and only four places outside that seam name a program.
2. **Decisions with a default.** 274 decisions were put to the person in six and a half weeks: 169 answered by
   them, 28 settled by their default with no one waiting, 49 closed by the agent as
   answered in conversation or moot, 28 open. Half of those closed were closed within 3 hours.
3. **Projects writing to each other.** The most-used commands are send and reply (2,059 runs). This week three
   reports from projects each named a real fault in colony, fixed the same day.
4. **One plan a project steers by.** The roadmap guide is the most-read text colony has (475 reads).
5. **Guidance said at its moment.** The idle wake, the line about open decisions and the refusal to wait on a
   clock each appear only when they apply. This is also what the research study found to work.
6. **Tests before every restart.** The board was restarted 128 times in two weeks while every project ran on it.

### What gets in the way

1. **One file does three jobs.** `board.py` is 12,419 lines: the store, the page (3,353 lines of it inside
   strings) and the web server (one 886-line method). Because everything needs the store, everything imports the
   page and server too; 633 imports sit inside functions to dodge the loops this makes, against 58 at the top.
   Its test file is 12,583 lines. Every change to colony pays for this in reading.
2. **The same instruction in several places.** The fresh reader found 13 instructions said twice or more (five
   wordings of "land your work"), and 9 places where two texts pull against each other.
3. **Small guides are seldom opened.** Two guides carry the reading (roadmap 475, fresh-eyes 294). The guide on
   asking was read 79 times against 969 decisions asked; the guide on writing to projects 42 times against 1,121
   messages. Whether agents remembered them or skipped them, the records cannot say.
4. **Parts built and not used, or not shown to pay.** Lessons: two proposed since it was built, none adopted.
   The research pass before an item: unproven either way. About 2,600 lines move or migrate things once; 773
   lines are phone audio trials living in the product.
5. **Churn.** A dozen command names in the records are gone or fading (one ran 1,285 times and 39 in the last
   two weeks). Removing is healthy; it also shows parts being built before their use was known.
6. **The lead's own load on the person.** One decision in nine was asked again in a new form. This week one
   question was put four times in a day, and a recommendation to build was reversed after a count that should
   have come first.

### What I would do differently

- **Split the big file, and nothing else structural.** A store with no upward imports, the page as real files,
  a table of routes. It adds no part; it is the one change both readers and the counts point to. It is a large
  mechanical change to a live board, so it would be done in steps behind the tests.
- **A pass of subtraction on the texts.** One wording for each instruction said twice. No merging of the small
  guides: unopened they cost nothing, merged they make every read longer.
- **Count before building, and once after.** The scripts behind this report took minutes and ran no model.
  Before a new part: how often does its moment occur in the records? Once it has been in use: is it used? This
  is how I would answer whether colony's work is getting better, with no new machinery.
- **Take out what is not product:** the audio trials and the one-time moves, to a folder outside the package.
- **Leave alone:** the storage, the consoles, the program seam, decisions with defaults, mail, the plan.

### Where the fresh readers and I differ

- One reader would merge the small guides and fold the helpers guide into the main one. I would not, for the
  reason above.
- The same reader sees the five lines at an item's start as pulling against the person's attention. I read them
  as what the person asked for; whether they are given is what the count on Nov 14 measures.
- One reader found no module unused, which is true of the code. The use counts add what code cannot show: a
  mechanism can be called from everywhere and still have no adoptions.
- Both marked several findings as guesses (the seven "tell the agent once" watchers as one pattern written seven
  ways; twenty modules with their own way of saving state). I have not verified those and list them as theirs.

### What this could not see

What the person uses on the board itself (Chat, reading aloud, the pages): nothing records it. Codex's sessions.
Whether the work the projects produce is better for any of this.
